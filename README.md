## Overview

Google Business Profile is a tool that allows eligible businesses to manage how their information appears on Google Search and Google Maps.

Businesses may encounter problems involving verification, ownership, profile suspensions, inaccurate information, duplicate listings, missing reviews, or visibility changes.

This repository provides a structured approach to investigating these issues and following Google's official procedures.

## Common GBP Problems

| Problem                           | Possible cause                                                 | Recommended action                                     |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Verification is incomplete        | Verification method or evidence requirements are unmet         | Follow the available verification process              |
| Profile is suspended              | Possible policy violation or account restriction               | Review policies and follow the appeal process          |
| Business listing already exists   | An existing profile may represent the same business            | Request access or investigate duplication              |
| Edit is rejected                  | Information may be inaccurate or inconsistent                  | Correct the information and review applicable policies |
| Reviews are missing               | Moderation, policy enforcement, or processing delays           | Review Google's review policies                        |
| Business information is incorrect | Profile changes or third-party data updates                    | Submit accurate corrections                            |
| Profile is difficult to find      | Search relevance, distance, prominence, or eligibility factors | Audit profile information and local SEO fundamentals   |
| Ownership request is pending      | Another owner currently manages the profile                    | Follow the official ownership request process          |

These symptoms do not necessarily indicate a technical error or policy violation. Investigate the specific circumstances before taking action.

## Step 1: Check Business Profile Eligibility

Before troubleshooting a listing, confirm that the business qualifies for a Google Business Profile.

Review the following:

* The business makes in-person contact with customers during its stated hours.
* The business operates at a legitimate location or qualifies as a service-area business.
* The business information accurately represents the real-world business.
* The person managing the profile is authorized to represent the business.
* The business name reflects its real-world name rather than additional promotional keywords.

Online-only businesses and certain other business types are not eligible.

Read Google's [Business Profile policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416) before creating a new profile.

### Recommended workflow

1. Confirm the business exists and operates as described.
2. Check whether a profile already exists.
3. Review the business name, address, and category.
4. Confirm that the business meets eligibility requirements.
5. Correct any inaccurate information before requesting verification.

## Step 2: Troubleshoot Verification Issues

Verification helps Google establish that an authorized person manages a business profile.

### Common symptoms

* Verification remains incomplete.
* A verification method is unavailable.
* Verification attempts do not succeed.
* The profile requires additional verification.
* The business cannot make certain edits before verification.

### Troubleshooting steps

1. Sign in to the Google Account that manages the profile.
2. Open the business profile through Google Search, Google Maps, or the official management interface.
3. Check the current verification status.
4. Follow the verification method Google offers.
5. Provide accurate and consistent business information.
6. Follow any additional instructions presented during verification.
7. If verification remains unavailable, consult the official Help Center.

Verification methods may vary by business, account, and location. Do not assume that every business can use phone, email, video, or postcard verification.

**Important:** Never share verification codes or account credentials with an unauthorized person.

Official resource: [Verify your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242)

## Step 3: Investigate Suspended or Disabled Profiles

A suspended or disabled profile may no longer appear publicly on Google Search or Maps.

Possible reasons include inaccurate business information, eligibility problems, policy violations, or restrictions affecting the associated Google Account.

### Investigation procedure

1. Sign in to the Google Account associated with the profile.
2. Check for account restrictions or security alerts.
3. Review Google's Business Profile policies.
4. Examine the business name, address, category, website, and other profile details.
5. Identify and correct any actual policy violations.
6. Gather supporting business documentation.
7. Use the official appeals process if the profile is eligible for appeal.
8. Monitor the appeal status and follow Google's instructions.

### Avoid these mistakes

* Creating another profile for the same business to bypass a suspension.
* Submitting repeated appeals before receiving a decision.
* Changing business information to something inaccurate.
* Using misleading documents.
* Paying an unofficial service that guarantees reinstatement.

A successful reinstatement cannot be guaranteed. The outcome depends on Google's review and the circumstances of the case.

Official resources:

* [Google Business Profile policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416)
* [Appeal Business Profile restrictions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551)

## Step 4: Resolve Ownership and Access Issues

Ownership conflicts occur when someone else manages an existing profile or the current owner cannot access the account.

### Recommended procedure

1. Search for the existing business listing on Google.
2. Confirm that the listing represents the correct business.
3. Sign in to the Google Account intended to manage the profile.
4. Request ownership or access through Google's official process.
5. Follow the instructions provided by Google.
6. Review owner and manager permissions after access is granted.
7. Remove outdated access only when authorized.

If the previous owner is unavailable, follow the official ownership request procedure instead of creating a duplicate profile.

### Access security checklist

* [ ] Confirm the primary owner.
* [ ] Review all managers and owners.
* [ ] Remove unauthorized users.
* [ ] Secure the associated Google Account.
* [ ] Enable two-step verification.
* [ ] Store recovery information securely.

Official resource: [Resolve duplicate profiles and ownership issues](https://support.google.com/business/answer/12756178)

## Step 5: Resolve Duplicate Listings

Multiple profiles for the same business can confuse customers and may violate Google's policies.

### Investigation procedure

1. Search Google Maps for the business name and address.
2. Compare the business names, addresses, phone numbers, and websites.
3. Determine whether the listings represent the same business or distinct eligible businesses.
4. Identify which profile is verified and actively managed.
5. Follow Google's procedure for requesting access, reporting duplicates, or requesting a merge where appropriate.
6. Confirm that the correct listing remains available after the issue is resolved.

### Important considerations

* Do not create a second profile simply because the original listing has problems.
* Do not assume two businesses at the same address are duplicates.
* Different services offered by the same business generally do not justify separate profiles.
* Businesses that relocate should follow Google's guidance for updating or replacing their existing listing, depending on the circumstances.

Official resource: [Resolve duplicate profiles and ownership issues](https://support.google.com/business/answer/12756178)

## Step 6: Troubleshoot Rejected or Pending Edits

Google may review business information changes before publishing them.

### Common affected fields

* Business name.
* Primary and secondary categories.
* Address or service area.
* Phone number.
* Website.
* Opening hours.
* Business description.
* Photos and other media.

### Troubleshooting steps

1. Open the profile and inspect the current published information.
2. Check the status of the proposed edit.
3. Verify that the new information is accurate.
4. Confirm that the change follows Google's business representation policies.
5. Review any notification explaining why the edit was rejected.
6. Correct inaccurate or unsupported information.
7. If necessary, follow the official appeal or support process.

Pending changes may require additional review. Avoid repeatedly submitting the same edit without investigating the underlying problem.

### Business name example

**Incorrect approach:** Adding location names, services, or marketing keywords to the business name when they are not part of the real-world business name.

**Correct approach:** Use the name that the business genuinely uses on its signage, website, and other real-world materials.

Official resource: [Google Business Profile policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416)

## Step 7: Investigate Missing Reviews

Customer reviews may be missing because of moderation, policy enforcement, delayed processing, or other review-related issues.

### Troubleshooting procedure

1. Confirm that the review is not visible on the public profile.
2. Check whether other recent reviews are also affected.
3. Review Google's prohibited and restricted content policies.
4. Avoid asking customers to change reviews in exchange for incentives.
5. Do not purchase reviews or use fabricated customer accounts.
6. Use Google's review management and support options when appropriate.
7. Monitor the issue rather than repeatedly submitting identical requests.

### Review policy reminders

* Do not offer rewards for positive reviews.
* Do not ask only satisfied customers to leave reviews in an attempt to manipulate ratings.
* Do not post reviews for your own business using fake identities.
* Do not threaten or pressure customers to remove legitimate criticism.
* Respond professionally to genuine customer feedback.

Not every missing review can be restored. Google determines whether content complies with its policies.

Official resource: [Manage customer reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)

## Step 8: Fix Incorrect Business Information

Accurate information helps customers understand where a business operates and how to contact it.

### Information to audit

| Field          | What to verify                                          |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business name  | Matches the real-world business name                    |
| Address        | Accurate and eligible for display                       |
| Service area   | Reflects the actual service area                        |
| Phone number   | Correct and controlled by the business                  |
| Website        | Points to the official business website                 |
| Business hours | Reflect actual operating hours                          |
| Categories     | Describe the business accurately                        |
| Description    | Provides factual information without prohibited content |
| Photos         | Represent the actual business and its services          |

### Recommended workflow

1. Compare the profile with the official business website and documentation.
2. Confirm any changes with the business owner.
3. Update only the fields that require correction.
4. Save the changes and review their publication status.
5. Recheck the public listing after Google processes the changes.

Google may receive information from multiple sources and may update profile details. Review any unexpected changes and correct inaccurate information through the available management tools.

## Step 9: Investigate Google Maps Visibility

A verified profile does not guarantee a particular ranking or placement in local search results.

Google's local results are generally influenced by relevance, distance, and prominence.

### Troubleshooting checklist

* [ ] Confirm that the profile is verified.
* [ ] Confirm that the profile is publicly available.
* [ ] Check the accuracy of the business name and category.
* [ ] Verify the address or service area.
* [ ] Confirm the website and phone number.
* [ ] Complete relevant business information.
* [ ] Review customer feedback and respond appropriately.
* [ ] Ensure that the website provides accurate, useful business information.
* [ ] Investigate duplicate profiles or policy restrictions.
* [ ] Compare results across different relevant searches and locations.

### Understand ranking differences

Local search results may vary depending on:

* The search query.
* The searcher's location.
* The business location.
* Competition in the area.
* The relevance and prominence of the business.

Avoid changing business names or creating additional profiles just to target more keywords.

**Note:** No legitimate troubleshooting process can guarantee a specific Google Maps position.

## Step 10: Prepare a Suspension Appeal

If Google provides an appeal option, prepare accurate supporting evidence before submitting it.

### Suggested preparation checklist

* [ ] Confirm the Google Account associated with the profile.
* [ ] Review the suspension notification.
* [ ] Identify the relevant policy or eligibility issue.
* [ ] Correct any actual violations.
* [ ] Verify that the business name and address are accurate.
* [ ] Gather applicable official business documents.
* [ ] Ensure that supporting evidence matches the profile.
* [ ] Submit the appeal using Google's official tool.
* [ ] Attach requested evidence within the stated submission window.
* [ ] Track the appeal status and wait for the decision.

Depending on the case, supporting evidence may include official business registration, a business license, tax documentation, or utility bills.

Submit only genuine documents. Do not alter documents to make them appear to support an inaccurate business address or identity.

Google advises against submitting multiple appeals for the same issue before receiving a decision. Do not create another profile for the same business while an appeal is under review.

Official resource: [Appeal Business Profile restrictions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551)

## Troubleshooting Checklist

Use this quick workflow when investigating a GBP issue.

1. **Identify:** Record the exact problem and when it started.
2. **Verify:** Confirm the correct profile and managing account.
3. **Inspect:** Review eligibility, account access, business information, and policy compliance.
4. **Document:** Save relevant notifications, screenshots, and supporting evidence securely.
5. **Correct:** Make only necessary and accurate changes.
6. **Escalate:** Use Google's official support or appeal process when needed.
7. **Monitor:** Check the profile status and public listing after the action.
8. **Prevent:** Document the resolution and review account security.

## Prevention Best Practices

### Account security

* Use unique passwords and two-step verification.
* Limit profile access to authorized people.
* Review permissions periodically.
* Remove access when a team member no longer needs it.
* Secure the Google Account associated with the profile.

### Profile management

* Keep business information accurate.
* Follow Google's eligibility and representation policies.
* Avoid unnecessary profile duplication.
* Document significant profile changes.
* Keep legitimate business records available.
* Use official Google support channels.

### Agency and third-party management

If managing profiles for clients:

* Obtain explicit authorization.
* Maintain transparent ownership arrangements.
* Give clients appropriate access to their profiles.
* Communicate proposed changes.
* Never promise guaranteed rankings or reinstatement.
* Never withhold profile access as leverage over a client.
